# P6 prep: SO-101 hardware backend, dry-run only
No motor has moved. Nothing here opens a serial port or imports LeRobot's robot
or motor code. Hardware numbers: **TBD (not measured)**. No ledger rows.

**What exists.** `SO101HardwareBackend` implements the same `Backend` protocol
as the MuJoCo backend, but only with `dry_run=True`; `dry_run=False` raises.
`send_targets` takes a target that already passed `SafetyController`. It maps
each sim joint (radians) into motor space: `sign * units_per_rad * q +
zero_offset`. Then it limits the command to the joint map's motor min/max, max
speed and max acceleration, braking with the same stopping rule as
`SafetyController` so it stops at its target without overshoot. It prints and
records the result. Nothing is sent. This limiter is **not yet a safety layer**
(see the blockers below). `read_state` returns the last commanded targets as a
`CommandedState` with `measured=False`; the recorder and the latency summary
refuse such state. The joint map (`configs/hardware/so101_joint_map.yaml`) fills in only
what LeRobot documents: motor names, ids 1-6 in spec order, and units (degrees
for the body, 0-100 for the gripper; `so_follower.py:50-60`,
`config_so_follower.py:42`). Every measured value is TBD. The loader refuses
TBD outside dry-run. In dry-run a TBD joint prints `unmapped` with its sim
target; it is never guessed.
`python -m xert hw-dry-run --spec xert/spec/so101.yaml --map
configs/hardware/so101_joint_map.yaml --clip <clip> --output <new dir>` runs
clip, calibrated mapper, safety layer, hardware clamp and print, in simulated
time. It writes `config.json` and `trace.jsonl`, both labelled "dry-run, no
hardware". The clip calibrates itself, as in P3.6.

**Why dry-run first.** A sign error or a wrong zero offset makes a real arm
slam into its own frame. A dry-run shows the exact numbers that would go to
each motor before any can move. It also proves that the hardware path never
loads the bus code (a test checks the imported modules).

**P6.2 BLOCKERS, before any motor moves.** Today the backend and the safety
layer each keep their own idea of where the arm is. (a) The backend never
learns of the e-stop or hold phase; it must hold its last sent value at zero
velocity (a strict-xfail test reproduces this through `run_loop`).
(b) `SafetyController` must take the applied hardware command, later the
measured position, as its state, so its watchdog return and rate limits act
on what the hardware was really sent. (c) Both must start from a read-only
`Present_Position` read, not the assumed rest pose.

**Owner-only bring-up, in order (P6.1-P6.2, Daniel present, a human on the
power switch from step 1):**
1. LeRobot motor setup (`lerobot-setup-motors`: writes ids and baud rate, one
   motor at a time) and calibration (`lerobot-calibrate`). Calibration runs
   `connect(calibrate=False)`, which calls `configure()`; its
   `torque_disabled()` block turns torque ON when it exits. Torque stays ON
   until `calibrate()` disables it for a new calibration, or until
   `disconnect()` turns it off at the end (the default).
2. Measure the joint map on the real arm: sign, scale, zero offset, limits,
   then choose speed and acceleration limits below the sim ones.
3. Read-only position reads: open the bus and call only
   `sync_read("Present_Position")`; compare with the arm's pose by hand.
4. Then one joint at a time, low speed, low torque limit, a human on the
   power switch. Never forward sim actions straight to the motors.

**LeRobot 0.6.1 calls that write to the bus** (avoid them in step 3).
`SOFollower.connect()` (`so_follower.py:92`) may call `calibrate()` and always
calls `configure()`. `configure()` (`:159`) writes operating mode, PID gains and
gripper torque limits, and its `torque_disabled()` block re-enables torque
when it exits (`motors_bus.py:677-691`). Other writers: `calibrate()` (`:115`),
`setup_motors()` (`:173`), `send_action()` (`:205`, `sync_write("Goal_Position")`),
`disconnect()` (torque off by default, `motors_bus.py:547`),
`disable_torque`/`enable_torque` (`feetech.py:291,302`), `write_calibration`
(`feetech.py:268`) and `configure_motors` (`feetech.py:209`).
`lerobot-find-joint-limits` drives the arm. Read-only:
`FeetechMotorsBus.connect()` (open port and ping), `read`, `sync_read`,
`read_calibration`, and `disconnect(disable_torque=False)`.

**Interview question:** Why clamp again in the hardware backend when the
safety controller already limits speed and acceleration?
**Answer:** The safety controller uses sim limits in joint radians; the motor
limiter will use measured, stricter limits in the motor's own units. It only
becomes a safety layer once blockers (a)-(c) make both share one state.
